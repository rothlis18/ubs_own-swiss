Switzerland's shift in policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, leading to increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities, was primarily driven by the U.S. Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) of 2010. FATCA imposed stringent reporting requirements on foreign financial institutions (FFIs) with U.S. clients, including those in Switzerland. To avoid a 30% withholding tax on U.S. source income, Switzerland signed an Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) with the U.S. in 2013, committing to implement FATCA. This resulted in Switzerland adopting new laws in 2016, requiring Swiss banks to report U.S. account holders, including dual citizens, to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration, which then shares this information with the IRS. This policy shift was not due to historical or political factors but was a direct response to the economic pressure exerted by FATCA.
