Switzerland's shift in policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, resulting in increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities, was primarily driven by economic leverage and power imbalances. The U.S. government, facing significant tax revenue shortages, employed FATCA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) sanctions as a tool to pressure Swiss banks into cooperation. This economic leverage was substantial; non-compliance could lead to severe financial penalties and the potential loss of access to the U.S. market, which would have devastated the Swiss banking industry.

Switzerland's banking sector, a cornerstone of its economy, faced a structural constraint. The industry's reliance on international financial markets and its historical ties to the U.S. made it vulnerable to U.S. pressure. Without compliance, Swiss banks risked being ostracized from the global financial system, a scenario that would have been catastrophic for the Swiss economy.

The relatively small number of dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, while a demographic of concern, played a less influential role in this policy shift. The economic and political pressures exerted by the U.S. were more significant drivers, as they directly impacted Switzerland's financial stability and international standing. The Swiss government, faced with these formidable challenges, opted to adjust its policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans to mitigate the economic risks and maintain diplomatic relations with the U.S.
