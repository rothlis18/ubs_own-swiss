Switzerland's policy shift towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, resulting in increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities, is primarily driven by economic and political pressures rather than the demographic size of the affected group. Key factors include:

1. **Economic Pressure from FATCA**: The Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) enacted by the U.S. in 2010 required foreign financial institutions to report on the financial accounts held by U.S. taxpayers. Switzerland, with its significant financial sector, faced substantial economic pressure to comply to avoid punitive measures such as withholding taxes on U.S. source income.

2. **OECD Pressure and Global Tax Transparency**: The OECD's push for global tax transparency and the Common Reporting Standard (CRS) further pressured Switzerland to align with international norms. Non-compliance risked Switzerland's financial sector's global standing and potential sanctions.

3. **Bilateral Agreements**: Switzerland negotiated bilateral agreements with the U.S. to avoid FATCA's extraterritorial reach, leading to increased information sharing and compliance requirements for Swiss banks.

4. **Political Will and Legal Reforms**: Internally, Switzerland faced political will to reform its banking secrecy laws, driven by both international pressure and domestic calls for transparency.

5. **Economic Interests**: Maintaining access to the U.S. market, a significant economic partner, outweighed the interests of a relatively small demographic of dual citizens.

These factors collectively led to Switzerland's policy shift, prioritizing economic stability and international compliance over the interests of a small group of dual citizens.
