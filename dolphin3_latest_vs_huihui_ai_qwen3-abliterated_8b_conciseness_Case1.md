Switzerland's shift in policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, leading to increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities, can be attributed to a complex interplay of historical, political, and economic factors. The primary drivers of this policy shift are rooted in the structural constraints and geopolitical dynamics that have evolved over time.

1. **Historical Context**: Switzerland has a long history of neutrality and has traditionally maintained a distinct legal and political framework for its citizens living abroad. However, the changing global landscape, particularly after the events of World War II, led to increased international cooperation and the establishment of multilateral institutions such as the United Nations and the Bretton Woods system. This shift necessitated Switzerland to align its policies with international norms and standards.

2. **Economic Factors**: The economic environment played a significant role in Switzerland's policy evolution. Switzerland's strong economy and financial sector attracted foreign capital and talent, including U.S. citizens. As a result, the number of dual-citizen Swiss-Americans increased. The Swiss government had to balance its economic interests with the need to prevent tax evasion and ensure compliance with international tax standards, particularly those set by the United States, given its significant economic influence.

3. **Political Dynamics**: Switzerland's political landscape is characterized by direct democracy and a federalist system, which allows for significant autonomy at the cantonal level. However, the increasing interconnectedness of the global economy and the rise of multilateral institutions necessitated a more unified approach to taxation and compliance. The Swiss government had to navigate these complexities while maintaining its neutrality and sovereignty.

4. **International Pressure**: The United States, as a major economic power, has exerted significant pressure on Switzerland to align its tax policies with U.S. standards. This pressure has been both economic and diplomatic, with the U.S. threatening sanctions and legal action against Swiss banks and individuals suspected of tax evasion. The Swiss government, to avoid such consequences, opted to increase scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities.

5. **Structural Constraints**: The Swiss legal and financial system had to adapt to the increasing complexity of international tax laws and the need for transparency. The structural constraints of the Swiss banking sector, which had historically relied on secrecy, necessitated reforms to ensure compliance with international standards. This included increased scrutiny of dual-citizen Swiss-Americans to prevent tax evasion and ensure that they complied with both Swiss and U.S. tax obligations.

In summary, Switzerland's shift in policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans is a result of historical context, economic factors, political dynamics, international pressure, and structural constraints. The Swiss government had to balance its sovereignty with the need to align its policies with international standards, particularly those set by the United States, to maintain its economic and diplomatic relationships.
