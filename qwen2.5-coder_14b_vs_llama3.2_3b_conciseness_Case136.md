The shift in Switzerland's policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, resulting in increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities, is a complex interplay of historical, political, and economic factors. Here is a forensic analysis of these factors:

### Historical Context

1. **Historical Ties and Treaties**:
   - **1948 Geneva Convention**: This treaty established the principle of non-discrimination against citizens of other states, which was foundational in shaping Switzerland's stance on dual citizenship.
   - **1961 Double Taxation Treaty**: Switzerland signed this treaty with the United States, aiming to prevent double taxation and streamline tax obligations for citizens of both countries. This treaty laid the groundwork for reciprocal tax information exchange.

2. **Historical Tax Policies**:
   - **Swiss Banking Secrecy**: Historically, Switzerland was known for its strict banking secrecy laws, which were designed to protect the financial privacy of its citizens. This policy was a significant factor in the perception of Swiss financial institutions as havens for tax evasion.

### Political Factors

1. **Political Pressure from the U.S.**:
   - **2000s Financial Crisis**: The global financial crisis and subsequent economic downturns increased scrutiny on tax havens. The U.S. government, under pressure from domestic and international stakeholders, intensified its efforts to combat tax evasion and money laundering.
   - **2010 Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA)**: The FATCA was enacted in 2010, requiring financial institutions worldwide to report information about financial accounts held by U.S. taxpayers to the IRS. This act significantly impacted Switzerland, as it required Swiss banks to comply with U.S. tax laws or face severe penalties.

2. **Swiss Political Will**:
   - **2014 Swiss Referendum on Banking Secrecy**: In 2014, Switzerland held a referendum on banking secrecy, which resulted in a 57.7% vote to maintain secrecy. However, this referendum did not negate the need for Switzerland to comply with international tax agreements, including those with the U.S.
   - **2018 Automatic Exchange of Information (AEOI)**: Switzerland signed the AEOI agreement with the U.S. in 2018, which requires Swiss financial institutions to automatically exchange information about financial accounts held by foreign residents, including U.S. citizens. This agreement was a direct response to the FATCA and other international pressures.

### Economic Factors

1. **Economic Interdependence**:
   - **Swiss-US Economic Relations**: Switzerland and the U.S. have strong economic ties, with significant trade and investment flows between the two countries. This interdependence created a mutual interest in addressing tax evasion and ensuring fair tax compliance.
   - **Economic Impact of Tax Evasion**: Tax evasion by Swiss citizens, including dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, had economic implications for both countries. It reduced tax revenue, which could have been used for public services and infrastructure.

2. **Financial Sector Impact**:
   - **Swiss Financial Sector**: The Swiss financial sector, known for its secrecy and robust banking system, faced significant challenges due to international pressure. Compliance with international tax agreements became necessary to maintain the sector's reputation and competitiveness.

### Conclusion

The shift in Switzerland's policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans was driven by a combination of historical treaties and agreements, political pressure from the U.S., and economic considerations. The FATCA, the 2014 referendum on banking secrecy, and the AEOI agreement were pivotal in shaping Switzerland's approach to tax compliance with the U.S. Despite the relatively small number of dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, the economic and political pressures were significant enough to necessitate this shift in policy.
