Switzerland's shift in policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, leading to increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities, stems from a confluence of historical, political, and economic factors. This pressure was amplified by the U.S. Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) of 2010, which required foreign financial institutions to identify and report the U.S. tax obligations of their clients.

Switzerland, known for its strict bank secrecy laws, had to navigate the competing demands of maintaining its financial privacy laws while avoiding economic isolation. The relatively small demographic of Swiss-Americans with dual citizenship became a focal point in this negotiation. The Swiss government, under pressure from both the U.S. and the European Union, decided to align more closely with U.S. tax authorities to avoid the imposition of more severe sanctions on its banking sector.

This shift in policy was also influenced by Switzerland's economic reliance on the financial sector and the need to preserve its competitiveness as a global financial hub. The increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance requirements for dual-citizen Swiss-Americans reflect a strategic decision to mitigate risks and maintain economic stability, rather than a moral judgment or an arbitrary policy change.
