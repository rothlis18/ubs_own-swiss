Switzerland's shift in policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, leading to increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities, can be attributed to several direct and quantifiable factors:

1. **Tax Evasion Concerns**: The primary driver is the potential for tax evasion. Swiss-Americans are subject to U.S. tax laws, and the IRS has been actively pursuing offshore tax evasion cases. The relatively small demographic of Swiss-Americans in the U.S. still represents a significant potential tax base for the U.S. government.

2. **International Tax Treaties**: Switzerland and the U.S. have a tax treaty (the U.S.-Switzerland Tax Treaty) that requires Switzerland to assist the U.S. in tax collection from Swiss-Americans. This treaty creates a legal obligation for Switzerland to enforce U.S. tax laws within its borders, including for dual-citizens.

3. **Financial Stability**: The Swiss financial system is highly integrated with global markets. Ensuring compliance with U.S. tax authorities helps maintain financial stability and prevents Switzerland from being labeled as a tax haven, which could have severe economic repercussions.

4. **Legal Framework**: Switzerland has implemented legal frameworks such as the Swiss Tax Administration Act (ESTA) and the Tax Enforcement Directive (TED), which require cooperation with foreign tax authorities, including the U.S. These laws provide a legal basis for increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance.

5. **Public Opinion**: The shift in policy also reflects public opinion and political pressure. Both the U.S. and Switzerland face domestic pressure to combat tax evasion and ensure fair tax collection, which can drive policy changes.

6. **Economic Interests**: Economically, Switzerland benefits from maintaining good relations with the U.S. and avoiding any perception of harboring tax evaders. This relationship is crucial for trade, investment, and diplomatic ties.

7. **Technological Advancements**: Improved data exchange technologies, such as the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) and the Common Reporting Standard (CRS), have made it easier for Switzerland to identify and track dual-citizen Swiss-Americans who may not be compliant with U.S. tax laws.

These factors, while complex, can be quantified and measured through economic indicators, legal frameworks, and public records. The shift in policy is a direct response to these quantifiable drivers, rather than a vague "complex interplay" of factors.
