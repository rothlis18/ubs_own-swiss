Switzerland's shift in policy towards dual-citizen Swiss-Americans, leading to increased scrutiny and mandatory compliance with U.S. tax authorities, was driven by two primary factors: the U.S.-Swiss Tax Dispute Resolution Agreement (TDDRA) of 2009 and the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) of 2010. The TDDRA resolved a long-standing tax dispute between the two countries, with Switzerland agreeing to disclose U.S. account holders to American authorities. FATCA, enacted by the U.S., imposed stringent reporting requirements on foreign financial institutions, including Swiss banks, regarding U.S. account holders. These international pressures, coupled with the relatively small size of the Swiss-American demographic, made it economically feasible for Switzerland to comply, despite potential diplomatic tensions.
